The push-up ladder

StepVersionNotes
1Wall push-upPalms flat at chest height, arms straight, one line from head to heel. Walk your feet back to make it harder.
2Incline push-upHands on a sturdy bench or table. The lower the surface, the harder it gets.
3Floor push-upHands just below shoulder level, feet together, gaze at the floor. About 2 seconds down, 1 second up, until elbows reach about 90 degrees.

Starter plan: 3 days a week (example)

2 to 3 sets of about 8 to 12 clean reps, using the hardest version you can do with good form. More than 12 easily? Move up one step. Cannot reach 8 clean reps? Move down one step.

Pull-up options (from Video 004, in review)

Four coaching options. No trial we found tested them as a ladder, so this is coaching practice, not proven science. Choose a version you can control, on equipment you trust. If you cannot verify the setup, skip that option.

OptionWhat it is
1 HangStraight arms, shoulder blades drawn down and back slightly. Count in seconds, stop before grip or form breaks.
2 RowPull your chest toward a secure support. We have no evidence this exact exercise gets beginners to a first pull-up faster.
3 Band-assistedA resistance band supports part of your weight. Follow the band's instructions and check band and bar before loading them.
4 Slow loweringReach the top from a stable step, lower as slowly as you can control. If you drop suddenly, it is too hard for now.

Example starter session (illustrative, not a tested prescription)

Two or three short hangs, then two or three sets of a pulling variation you can control. Rest until you can keep good form. Add slow lowering only if you can control the descent.

4-week tracker

Tap a day to mark it done. Twelve sessions is a suggestion: resting or skipping a day never resets or penalises anything.
